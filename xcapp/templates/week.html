How points and the score work

Points come from the posted team workouts: each workout minute earns points, harder minutes more ({% for z, r in rates.items() %}{{ z|lower }} {{ r }}{{ ', ' if not loop.last else '' }}{% endfor %} a minute). The target is the planned points of the week’s posted workouts, so it grows as workouts are added. Each workout earns up to its planned points: running it faster, adding miles, or running on other days earns no extra points (miles count in the Miles column, and running days in the score). Score: points against the target, miles against the group’s weekly goal, and days with at least {{ min_minutes }} minutes run (target {{ target_days }}), each counted up to 100%. This week ({{ mix.label|lower }}): {{ (100 * mix.points)|round|int }}% points, {{ (100 * mix.miles)|round|int }}% miles, {{ (100 * mix.days)|round|int }}% days. The mix follows the week’s phase in the season plan: more miles in base weeks, more workout points in season. Without a points target or a miles goal, the other parts share its weight.

Green 90% or more, amber 80 to 89%, red below 80%. Each week has {{ target_days }} run days{% if target_days < 7 %} and {{ 7 - target_days }} rest day{{ 's' if target_days < 6 }} ({{ weekdays[rest_day] }} first){% endif %}. Each day is judged on its own: a workout day against the workout’s planned miles and points; an easy day against an equal share of what the workouts leave (the weekly miles goal and points target, less the workouts, ÷ the other run days). A run on a rest day is compared with an easy day. The Miles column is the week so far against the miles due so far: the weekly goal ÷ the run days gives miles per day, added for each run day through today, so the full goal is due by the last run day (the rest day adds nothing). The Points column is the week so far against the posted workouts through today. The Score column’s color shows whether the athlete is on track for the week: the same mix of points, miles and days, measured against what’s due so far. On workout days, Pace and HR are the share of the workout’s rep time spent in the target pace zone and heart-rate zone (same colors); open the run for the chart. Tick “Show why” or point at a number to see the sums. Each group’s team workouts are on its group row.

Miles, points and heart rate come from synced runs; effort and pain from what the athlete reported. Flags are for the coach and don’t change the score: easy days run faster than easy pace, points up more than 10% on the week before, and treadmill runs whose watch pace may be off.

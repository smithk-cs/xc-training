How hard was each race?

Each runner’s track benchmark (their best VDOT from the meets ticked below) predicts what they’d run at any distance on a flat track at 55°F or cooler, at sea level. A race’s difficulty is how much slower than that our runners actually ran (the median, so one great or bad day doesn’t decide it). The app’s estimate is what its course and weather settings account for. The gap between them is what’s left unexplained: if it stays near zero across races, adjusted times line up with track fitness.

How it works

  • Benchmark to prediction: Daniels and Gilbert’s oxygen-cost and endurance equations (the VDOT tables in Daniels’ Running Formula) turn a track time into a VDOT, and a VDOT into a predicted time at any distance, so a 3200 can be compared with a 5K or 3 miles.
  • Weather and altitude: heat at about 0.15% per °F above 55°F and altitude from Daniels’ Tables 6.1 and 6.2; dew point tiers are the app’s starting values. Pressure is recorded for reference only.
  • Course: the rest of the slowdown, in seconds per mile. Published lab studies put soft sand at about 50% more energy than grass and uneven ground at about 5% more than smooth, so measured values from your own runners are the better guide.
  • Variation: medians, the middle-50% spread, results more than {{ K.OUTLIER_PCT|int }}% off set aside, at least {{ K.MIN_RESULTS }} results before suggesting a setting, and blending with the current setting. This is the same idea results-based course ratings use: compare many runners’ times on a course with what they run elsewhere.
